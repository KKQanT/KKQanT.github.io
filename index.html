gwork

A small, auditable command-line tool that reads and edits your own Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive files and Gmail drafts from your own computer.

What it does

gwork is a Python command-line program. You run it on your own machine, you sign in with your own Google account, and it acts on the files you point it at. It exists so that a person, or a coding assistant working on that person's behalf, can do ordinary Workspace work without clicking through a browser: read a document, fill in a spreadsheet, build a slide deck from a template, draft an email, or read the review comments someone left on a file.

Typical uses:

  • Read a Google Doc or Sheet and summarise it
  • Create or edit a Slides deck on a company template
  • List, search, upload, download or copy files in Drive
  • Draft a Gmail message for you to review and send yourself
  • Read and reply to reviewer comments on a Doc, Sheet or deck

What it is not

There is no gwork website, no gwork account, and no gwork server. Nothing you do with it passes through a service we operate, because there isn't one. The program runs on your computer and talks directly to Google's APIs using the permission you granted it.

The permissions it asks for

When you sign in, Google will show you the list below and ask whether to allow it. gwork requests these because the commands it offers cover all five services. It only ever touches the specific file or message you name in a command.

PermissionWhy
Google DriveList, search, read, upload, download, copy and organise your files
Google DocsRead and edit documents
Google SheetsRead and edit spreadsheets
Google SlidesRead and build presentations
GmailSearch and read your mail, and create drafts. gwork does not send mail.
Email addressSo the tool can tell you which account you are signed in as

You can withdraw all of it at any time from your Google Account permissions page.

Where your data goes

Nowhere except between your computer and Google. Your sign-in token is written to a file on your own machine, readable only by you. It is never transmitted anywhere, never shared between users, and never seen by anyone else. The privacy policy sets this out in full.

The unverified app warning

Google shows a screen saying "Google hasn't verified this app" when you sign in. That is expected. It means this tool has not been through Google's paid verification review, not that anything is wrong with it. Google reserves that review for apps distributed to the general public; gwork is an internal tool used by a small number of named people. Choose Advanced, then Go to gwork (unsafe) to continue.
